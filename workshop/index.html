Why it fits university preparation: admissions teams and selective programs look for evidence of what a student has made, not only grades. A live portfolio is that evidence, and it grows with every later session.

The 30-minute flow

Short build cycles with a visible result in each block.

0–5

Why a portfolio

I show the finished sample page and explain how universities and programs look at what students have built, not just their grades.

5–12

AI as a co-writer

Students tell me about themselves, and I prompt an AI tool live on the projector to draft an "about me" section and suggest a page design. They learn to give clear instructions and to edit the AI's output instead of accepting it as is.

The AI tool runs only on my screen, so students don't need their own AI accounts.

12–22

Make it yours

Students copy the ready-made template on GitHub and customize it in the browser: their name, interests, colours, and one thing they're proud of. Numbered markers in the file show exactly what to change. No installs needed.

22–28

Go live

They publish the site with GitHub Pages and share the link with the group.

28–30

What's next

What we'll add over the coming sessions: a first app, a project write-up, and awards or activities.

Same workshop, three levels

Every age group makes a page about themselves. What changes is who does the technical steps and how much is explained. GitHub accounts require age 13+, so younger students never need one.

Ages 8–10

"All about me" page

Play first, technology second. Students design; I build.

AI idea
"AI is a helper that has read lots of examples and guesses what comes next." We play Bossy Robot: kids give me instructions to draw a smiley face, and I follow them literally. Vague instructions give funny results, which teaches why clear prompts matter.
Make it
Each child fills in a paper card: first name, a favourite emoji, three favourite things, one proud moment. They pick their colour from swatches.
Go live
I type their cards into the template on the projector and publish one class gallery page. Each child sees their own page appear.

What's needed

Students bring

  • A laptop with a web browser
  • Ages 13+: a GitHub account, created beforehand with parental consent. I send a one-page setup guide.

I prepare

  • The portfolio template and a step-by-step handout (already done)
  • A short parent note explaining the tools and accounts (draft below)
  • Paper "About me" cards and a classroom account for students under 13
Online safety rule for every age: pages are public, so students use their first name only. No last names, school names, photos of themselves, or contact details.

GitHub account setup guide (ages 13+, at home with a parent)

  1. Go to github.com and click Sign up.
  2. Use a parent-approved email address.
  3. Pick a username that isn't your full name, for example alex-builds. It appears in your website link.
  4. Choose the free plan and confirm your email.
  5. Bring your username and password to the workshop.

Running a trial session

A checklist for the first run with a small group of 4–8 students.

One week before

  • Agree on date, room, age group, and group size
  • Send the parent note and the GitHub setup guide (ages 13+)
  • Check that the room's Wi-Fi allows github.com and *.github.io (some school networks block them)
  • Set up the TRNT classroom GitHub account for students under 13

Two days before

  • Confirm students have created accounts; collect usernames
  • Run the full student flow myself on a fresh test account, from copying the template to a live link
  • Print the handout and the "About me" cards

On the day (arrive 20 minutes early)

  • Test the projector and the AI tool, and open the sample page
  • Write the Wi-Fi password and the template link on the board
  • Have a backup: if a student can't log in, they pair with a neighbour or use the classroom account
  • Remind everyone of the first-name-only rule before they start typing

During the session

  • Keep to the five time blocks and use a visible timer
  • GitHub Pages can take 1–5 minutes to go live, so start publishing at minute 22, not later
  • Note where students get stuck; that becomes next session's fixes

Afterwards

  • Collect every student's live link
  • Ask three questions: What did you make? What was hardest? What do you want to build next?
  • Send TRNT a one-page summary: attendance, links published, feedback, and changes for the next run

Draft parent note

Short, plain, and covers the questions parents usually ask.

Dear parent or guardian,

In our first AI & Innovation session, your child will build a simple personal website and publish it online. It will become a portfolio where they collect the projects they make with us, which they can later use in university and program applications.

Tools: GitHub, a free website used by professional software developers, and an AI writing tool that only the instructor uses, on the classroom screen.

Accounts: students aged 13 and over need a free GitHub account, created at home with you (setup guide attached). Students under 13 do not need any account; the instructor publishes their page.

Privacy: the website is public, so students use their first name only. We do not include last names, school names, photos, or contact details. You can ask us to take a page down at any time.

Questions are welcome at any time.
