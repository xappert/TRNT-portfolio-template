From classroom to company

A Student Product Incubator: real teams, real products, real measures.

Students join a TRNT product team that matches their level, or propose their own product. Every product moves through the same pipeline, with clear evidence required at each stage. The ones with real potential get TRNT's help to become products people pay for.

Two ways in

Some students want to join something real right away. Others already have an idea. Both are welcome, and both paths meet in the same pipeline.

🧩

Join a product team

  • TRNT keeps a pool of live products, for example from TRNT itself, partner companies, or non-profits.
  • Students are placed in a role that matches their proficiency and grow into bigger roles over time.
  • They learn how real product teams work: roadmaps, users, deadlines, and shipping.
💡

Propose your own product

  • Any student or team can pitch an idea at an Idea Review.
  • The AI Startup Studio workshop is the on-ramp: problem, customers, prototype, pitch.
  • Approved ideas get a team, a mentor, and a place in the pipeline.

↓ Both paths enter the same pipeline, with the same success criteria ↓

How students are placed

Placement is based on evidence, not guesswork. The workshops already produce that evidence: a portfolio of real projects.

Roles on a product team

Product leadAI builderDesignerUser researcherData & testingGrowth & marketing

Ages 8–12 take part as junior testers and idea scouts. They try products, give feedback, and suggest problems worth solving, which keeps the pipeline full and gets them ready for a team later.

L3

The incubator pipeline

Six stages. Each ends in a gate, a short review where the team shows evidence and a panel of TRNT staff and mentors decides whether to advance, iterate, or park it.

0 · Idea

1–2 weeks
Team producesA one-page pitch: the problem, who has it, and why it matters.GateIs the problem real and specific? Is the team committed?

1 · Discover

3–4 weeks
Team produces10+ user interviews and a problem statement backed by quotes.GateDo most interviewees confirm the problem matters?

2 · Prototype

4–6 weeks
Team producesA working AI prototype, tested with 5+ real users.GateCan users complete the core task? Would they use it again?

3 · Pilot

6–8 weeks
Team producesA product used by a real group, such as a school, club, or partner.GateDo people keep using it? Will someone pay or sponsor it?

4 · Launch

1 term
Team producesPricing, a business model, and a launch plan, built with TRNT.GateTRNT's investment review: commit resources to sell it?

5 · Grow

Ongoing
Team producesRevenue, growth, and new versions.OutcomeA product run by TRNT, a spin-out company, or a licence to a partner.

Success criteria you can measure

Every gate uses a weighted scorecard, so decisions are consistent across teams, mentors, and locations, and products can be ranked fairly. Try it: pick a gate, score a sample team, and see the decision.

Decision rules (illustrative)
4.0+ and no criterion below 3: advance
2.5–3.9, or any criterion below 3: iterate
Below 2.5: park, or pivot to a new idea

Measuring students, not just products

A product can fail while the student succeeds. A separate growth scorecard tracks each student every term. It's rated by a mentor, by teammates, and by the student, and it drives placement and level changes.

DimensionWhat we look for
CraftThe quality of their work in their role: code, design, research, or growth
OwnershipTakes responsibility for outcomes and follows through without being chased
CollaborationMakes the team better: shares, helps, and handles disagreement well
CommunicationExplains decisions clearly in writing, in reviews, and at Demo Day
Learning speedHow quickly they improve after feedback
Responsible AIChecks AI output, protects user data, and is honest about limits
Rankings with care. Products are ranked openly at every gate. Student scores and rankings are used for placement and leadership selection, and are shared privately with the student and their family. Public recognition happens through Demo Day awards and gate milestones, never public leaderboards of children.

From student product to commercial product

🏢 What TRNT provides

  • Mentors from industry and universities at every gate
  • Engineering and design support to make a prototype production-ready
  • Legal setup, pricing, and go-to-market help
  • Pilot partners: schools, clubs, and partner organisations
  • Funding or resources for products that pass the launch gate

🎓 What students gain

  • Ownership of the code they write, which they can take with them down the road
  • Credit as founders or contributors on a real, live product
  • A share in the success, on terms agreed with families in advance
  • Gate milestones and product metrics for university applications
  • An early, honest read on their entrepreneurial strengths
  • A mentor network that lasts beyond the program

Open questions to agree together

These would shape the details. I'd suggest settling them with TRNT's leadership and legal advisers before the first cohort.

Ownership and IPStudents own the code they write. What rights does TRNT hold in a product it helps commercialise, and how does that differ for a product from TRNT's pool?
Sharing in successRevenue share, equity, or a prize? How is it handled for students under 18, with parental agreement?
The product poolWhich products go in: TRNT's own, partner companies, non-profits? Who maintains the pool?
Gate panelsWho sits on them, and how are mentors from different locations kept consistent?
Time commitmentHow many hours per week on a team, and how does it fit around school and exams?
Data and privacyWhat rules apply to products that collect user data, especially data from other young people?
