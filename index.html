Students learn AI by building things they actually want.

The workshops

Each one stands on its own and ends with something students can show at home. Taken together, they form a path from using AI to building it.

🤖Ages 8–18

Build Your Own AI Helper

Students design an AI assistant for a real person in their life, such as a study buddy for a sibling or a recipe helper for a grandparent, then test it with them and improve it.

They learn: clear instructions, product thinking, and safety rules they write themselves.

Try the builder Concept
📷Ages 8–18

Invent a Smart Gadget

Students train their own image, sound, or pose model and turn it into an invention: a recycling sorter, a posture coach, a sign-language reader.

They learn: how AI learns from data, why it fails, and how to make it fairer.

Concept
🦾Ages 13–18

Build Your Own Jarvis

Students build a personal AI agent with a personality they write, a memory they control, skills they teach it, and tasks it runs on a schedule.

They learn: how AI agents work inside, and the trust decisions behind them.

Concept
🌐

Running through all of them: a portfolio of their own

In a 30-minute first session, students publish a personal website. Every project they build afterwards gets added to it, so it becomes evidence of real work for university and program applications.

About these ideas

I build AI products for a living. At Xappert, our product work starts from what users need and then tests whether the solution actually works for them, and I run my own Jarvis, a voice-activated AI agent built on Hermes, day to day. These workshops give students the same process at their level: find a real person, solve a real problem, test it, and explain it.

The concepts are a starting point for discussion. Once we agree on a direction, I'm happy to shape the teaching format, session length, and materials around it.
